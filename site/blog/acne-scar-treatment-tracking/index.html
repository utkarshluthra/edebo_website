Acne Scar Treatment: How to Track Progress Across Sessions

A practical guide for dermatologists on documenting acne scar treatment across multiple sessions with standardised imaging, texture scores and 3D surface records.

Acne scar treatment is a multi-session commitment: microneedling, lasers, subcision, peels or combinations, spread over months. Improvement is real but gradual, and patients who cannot see it tend to stop halfway. Good documentation is what keeps them on the programme long enough to benefit.

Why scars are hard to document

Atrophic acne scars (ice pick, boxcar and rolling) are depressions in the skin. What we see is mostly their shadow. That makes them extremely sensitive to lighting:

  • Flat frontal light hides scars.
  • Low-angle side light reveals them, sometimes dramatically.

If session one is photographed under side light and session five under a ring light, the patient appears to have improved hugely. Reverse it and they appear to have got worse. Neither is honest. See why 2D before-and-after photos mislead.

A documentation protocol for scar programmes

Fix the lighting

Choose one lighting setup and never change it. Many dermatologists use a consistent oblique light for scar documentation because it reveals texture; the key is consistency, not the specific choice.

Fix the views

Frontal plus both obliques, with the head level. Add close-ups of the worst-affected areas at a fixed distance.

Bare, clean skin

No makeup, no moisturiser shine, no recent active treatment redness if avoidable. Record the date of the last session.

Capture at every session

Image before each session, not after. Post-procedure redness and swelling distort the record.

Add measured texture

Consistent skin analysis texture scores, taken under the same conditions, turn a subjective impression into a trend. Edebo returns a skin profile and skin analysis report, including texture, with every scan at no extra cost. See in-clinic skin analysis explained.

Add 3D for rolling scars and contour

Broad rolling scars and volume loss after acne affect the contour of the cheek. A 3D scan captures that contour and can show change after subcision or filler. See measuring aesthetic results in millimetres.

Matching the record to the scar type

Different scar types respond to different treatments, and they also show up differently in images:

Scar type Appearance Best documented with
Ice pick Narrow, deep pits Close-up images, texture scores
Boxcar Wider depressions with defined edges Oblique-light images, texture scores
Rolling Broad, sloping undulations 3D contour, oblique views
Hypertrophic Raised scars 3D contour, close-ups

Most patients have a mix, so a combined record of standard views, texture scores and 3D contour covers the range. Noting which types dominate at baseline also helps explain why some areas improve faster than others.

Grading alongside imaging

Clinical grading scales remain the standard way to describe scar severity. Use your preferred scale at baseline and at milestones, and record it next to the images and texture scores. Imaging is a measurement and visualisation tool; the grade and the treatment plan stay with the clinician.

Talking to patients about progress

  • Set expectations at the start: improvement, not erasure, is the realistic aim for most scars. See managing patient expectations.
  • At each session, show the baseline and current images side by side under identical conditions.
  • Show the texture score trend, not just a single number.
  • Celebrate measurable improvement, and be honest when a session showed little change.

Pigmentation after acne

Post-inflammatory hyperpigmentation often accompanies scars, especially in Indian skin types. Track pigmentation separately so patients understand which concern is improving. See pigmentation and melasma tracking.

KPI to watch

Programme completion rate: patients who complete the planned number of sessions divided by those who start. If structured progress reviews raise this number, they are paying for themselves. More in aesthetic clinic patient retention.

Frequently asked questions

Why is acne scar improvement hard to see in photos?

Atrophic scars are shallow depressions defined by shadow. Frontal or flat lighting hides them; side lighting exaggerates them. Different lighting between sessions can make progress look worse or better than it is.

How many sessions do acne scar treatments usually need?

Most acne scar protocols involve several sessions spaced weeks apart, and improvement is gradual. That makes consistent documentation at each session important.

Can skin analysis measure acne scars?

Skin analysis texture scores can track changes in surface texture over time when capture is consistent. They support, but do not replace, clinical grading of scars.
