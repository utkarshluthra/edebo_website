Ethical Aesthetic Clinic Marketing With Real Patient Results

How Indian aesthetic clinics can market real patient results responsibly: separate consent, honest before-and-after images and factual claims.

Social media has made before-and-after images the currency of aesthetic marketing. It has also made misleading images common: different lighting, flattering angles, filters, cherry-picked results. Patients increasingly know this, which means honest marketing is not just ethical; it is a competitive advantage.

This article covers practical principles. It is not legal advice; check the professional and advertising rules that apply to your practice, which can change.

Principle 1: Consent comes first, and separately

Clinical consent to photograph a patient does not cover marketing. For any external use, record separate, specific consent that states:

  • which images will be used,
  • where (website, Instagram, print, presentations),
  • whether the face will be visible or cropped,
  • how long the consent lasts,
  • how to withdraw it.

Under India's DPDP Act, consent must be free, specific, informed and capable of being withdrawn. See patient consent for clinical photography and the DPDP Act and patient photos.

Principle 2: Like for like images

A before-and-after pair is only honest if the conditions match: same lighting, angle, distance, expression, makeup and hair. Our guide on why 2D before-and-after photos mislead lists the common distortions. If your "after" was shot in better light, reshoot it or do not use it.

Principle 3: No editing

Do not retouch, filter or smooth clinical images used to show results. Cropping for privacy is fine; altering the result is not. Turn off beauty modes on clinic devices.

Principle 4: Context with every result

State the treatment, the number of sessions and the time since treatment. Results vary between individuals; say so plainly rather than in tiny print.

Principle 5: Represent typical outcomes

Showing only your single best result implies it is typical. Use a range of representative results, and avoid language such as "guaranteed" or "permanent" unless it is clinically accurate.

Principle 6: Be careful with claims

Avoid superlatives you cannot support, comparisons with named competitors, and medical claims beyond what the treatment does. For imaging specifically, describe it as measurement and visualisation, not diagnosis.

How 3D imaging helps honest marketing

  • Aligned comparisons remove angle and pose as variables, so a 3D before-and-after is harder to fake by accident.
  • Measured change gives you a factual way to describe a result: "about 1 ml of midface volume restored, measured at two weeks."
  • Rotatable views show results from every side instead of one flattering angle.
  • Branded reports: on Edebo's Premium plan, your own brand kit appears on reports and shared views, and social media integration helps share consented results consistently.

Content ideas that build trust without patient images

Not every post needs a patient. Educational content performs well and carries no consent risk:

  • explain how a treatment works,
  • show how you measure results,
  • answer common questions,
  • explain safety and consent practices,
  • share how you set expectations; see managing patient expectations.

A checklist before posting any patient result

  1. Separate marketing consent recorded and current?
  2. Matched conditions between before and after?
  3. No editing or filters?
  4. Treatment, sessions and timeframe stated?
  5. Representative, not just the best case?
  6. Claims factual and supportable?
  7. Face cropped or included per consent?

Honest marketing builds the kind of trust that turns followers into patients and patients into referrers. It also connects directly to consultation conversion.

Frequently asked questions

Can aesthetic clinics post patient before-and-after photos?

Only with clear, specific, documented consent for that use, and in line with applicable professional and advertising rules. Clinical consent alone does not cover marketing use.

What makes a before-and-after image misleading?

Different lighting, angle, distance, makeup or expression between images, editing or filters, choosing an unrepresentative result, or omitting the timeframe and treatment details.

How can 3D imaging support honest marketing?

Aligned 3D comparisons remove pose and lighting tricks, and measured change gives a factual basis for describing results, provided the patient has consented to marketing use.
