Documenting Botulinum Toxin Results: Static and Dynamic Views

How clinics can document neuromodulator treatment consistently: baseline and review timing, animation views, brow position and 3D records for lower-face slimming.

Neuromodulator treatments are among the most common in aesthetic practice and among the most frequently repeated. That makes good documentation valuable twice over: once to show the result, and again to plan the next dose. Yet many clinics capture only a quick neutral photo, which misses most of what the treatment does.

Why neuromodulator results need dynamic views

Neuromodulators mainly change movement. A neutral face may look almost identical before and after, while the frown, forehead lines and crow's feet during expression change markedly. Documenting only the neutral face undersells the result.

The standard views

At baseline and review, capture the same set:

View Instruction Shows
Neutral "Relax your face, lips together, eyes open" Static lines, brow position
Frown "Frown as if you're cross" Glabellar lines
Brow raise "Raise your eyebrows as high as you can" Forehead lines
Squint / smile "Smile broadly" or "Squeeze your eyes shut" Crow's feet
Clench (if relevant) "Bite your back teeth together" Masseter bulk

Use the same words every time. Different instructions produce different expressions and make comparison unreliable. Our standardised photo protocol covers lighting and positioning.

Timing

  • Baseline: before injection, before any marking.
  • Review: commonly around two weeks, when the effect is usually established.
  • Pre-retreatment: before the next session, to see how much effect remains. This helps tailor the next plan.

Record the interval since treatment with every image.

Brow position and symmetry

Neuromodulators can raise or lower brow position and change brow shape. Measuring brow height relative to a fixed landmark at baseline and review documents both the intended change and any asymmetry. Pre-existing brow asymmetry is common; record it before treatment so it is not attributed to the injection later. See facial symmetry analysis.

Lower-face slimming

Masseter treatment changes lower-face contour gradually over weeks. This is a shape change, so 3D scans are well suited: align baseline and review scans and show the change in lower-face width as a colour map. Keep teeth lightly together and the expression neutral in both scans. See jawline and chin contouring.

Using records to plan the next dose

A record of how the patient responded, and how long the effect lasted, is the best guide for the next session. Clinics that review before retreatment can adjust the plan instead of repeating the last one by habit.

Turning one-off patients into regulars

Neuromodulator patients who see a clear, measured record of what changed are more likely to return on schedule. Pair the review with a reminder system tied to their own results; see tracking patient progress across follow-up visits and aesthetic clinic patient retention.

Capturing expressions consistently

Animation views are only comparable if the expression is equally strong in both images. A few practical habits help:

  • Demonstrate the expression yourself before asking the patient, so they know what you mean.
  • Ask for maximum effort each time ("frown as hard as you can") rather than a casual expression, because maximum effort is easier to repeat.
  • Capture two attempts and keep the stronger one, at baseline and at review.
  • Keep the head still and level; it is common for patients to tilt their head back when raising their brows.
  • Use the same order of expressions every time, so fatigue affects both sessions equally.

Static, dynamic and shape changes

It helps to think of neuromodulator outcomes in three groups, each documented differently:

Outcome Example Best record
Static change Softer resting forehead lines Neutral photo or scan
Dynamic change Reduced frown lines during expression Animation views
Shape change Slimmer lower face, brow lift Aligned 3D scans

Most clinics already record the first, some record the second, and few record the third well. Covering all three gives patients a complete picture of what their treatment did and gives the clinician a better basis for adjusting the next session.

Consent for animation images

Expression images can feel more personal than neutral ones. Include them explicitly in imaging consent, and treat any marketing use as a separate decision. See patient consent for clinical photography.

Frequently asked questions

When should botulinum toxin results be reviewed?

Many clinics review at around two weeks, when the effect is usually established, and record again before the next treatment to see how long the effect lasted.

What views should be captured for neuromodulator treatment?

A neutral face plus the relevant animations, such as frowning, raising the eyebrows and smiling or squinting, captured the same way at baseline and review.

Can 3D imaging show results of masseter treatment?

Yes. Aligned 3D scans can show change in lower-face width and contour after masseter treatment, provided teeth position and expression are consistent.
