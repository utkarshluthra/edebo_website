Lip Filler Assessment and Planning With 3D Imaging

How 3D imaging helps clinicians assess lip proportions, symmetry and projection before lip filler, and show patients a measured, natural-looking result.

Lip filler is one of the most requested aesthetic treatments, and one of the most sensitive to small changes. A millimetre of extra projection can be the difference between natural and overdone. It is also an area where patients often arrive with a reference photo and unrealistic expectations. 3D imaging helps with both.

Why lips are tricky in 2D

Lips are small, curved and highly mobile. In a frontal photo, projection is invisible. In profile, a slight head tilt or a relaxed versus pursed mouth changes everything. And because lips are so expressive, comparing a "before" and "after" taken with slightly different expressions is common. See why 2D before-and-after photos mislead.

What a 3D lip assessment shows

On a 3D model, the clinician can look at the lips from every angle at once and measure:

  • Upper-to-lower lip ratio: many clinicians use a ratio where the lower lip is fuller than the upper as a natural reference, but individual and ethnic variation is wide.
  • Lip width and how it relates to the distance between the pupils or the nose width.
  • Projection in profile, relative to the nose and chin.
  • Cupid's bow definition and philtral column shape.
  • Symmetry between left and right halves. Most people have some asymmetry; documenting it before treatment protects both patient and clinician. See facial symmetry analysis.

A 3D workflow for lip filler

  1. Baseline scan with lips relaxed and gently closed, before any numbing cream.
  2. Consultation on the model: rotate to profile, show the current ratio and any asymmetry, and agree the goal in plain terms. If the patient brings a reference image, use the model to explain how their own proportions differ.
  3. Annotate planned areas of enhancement and any asymmetry to correct.
  4. Review scan at about two weeks after swelling settles.
  5. Compare and measure: show the volume colour map, the change in projection and the improvement in symmetry.

Managing expectations

Lip consultations often fail not on technique but on expectation. A patient who wanted a dramatic change may feel underwhelmed by a subtle, balanced result, even when it is exactly what was planned.

The 3D baseline helps here. Showing the measured change, side by side with the original, reminds the patient where they started. It also supports the conversation about building volume gradually over sessions rather than all at once. Our guide on managing patient expectations covers the conversation in detail.

Building volume across sessions

Many clinicians prefer to build lip volume gradually, especially for first-time patients. 3D records make that approach easier to explain and to follow:

  • At the first review, show the measured change and how close the patient is to the agreed goal.
  • Use the same baseline for every later session, so the patient sees cumulative change, not just the latest step.
  • Track symmetry at each step; small corrections are easier than large ones.
  • Record how much of the change has held at later reviews to inform maintenance timing.

Patients who can see a cumulative, measured path towards their goal are usually more comfortable with a gradual approach, which tends to give more natural results.

Safety and documentation

  • Record consent for imaging separately from treatment consent; see patient consent for clinical photography.
  • Keep the baseline and review scans in the patient record. If a patient later questions the result or raises a complication, a dated, measured record is far stronger than memory.
  • Be clear that imaging is a measurement and visualisation tool. It does not replace clinical assessment of anatomy and vascular risk.

For clinics building a lip programme

Lips often lead to repeat visits, both for maintenance and for complementary treatments. Measured, visible progress makes those conversations easier and more honest. For the broader filler workflow, read dermal filler volume measurement with 3D imaging.

Frequently asked questions

What measurements matter in lip assessment?

Common ones include upper-to-lower lip height ratio, lip width, vermilion show, projection in profile, cupid's bow shape and left-right symmetry.

Can 3D imaging show lip asymmetry before treatment?

Yes. Mirroring and measuring the left and right halves of the lips on a 3D model shows existing asymmetry, which should be documented and discussed before treatment.

Does 3D imaging predict how my lips will look?

No. 3D imaging records the current shape and measures change after treatment. It is not a prediction. Any simulated preview should be clearly labelled as an illustration.
