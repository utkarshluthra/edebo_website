Dermal Filler Volume Measurement With 3D Imaging

How clinicians use 3D imaging to plan and measure dermal filler results: baseline scans, volume colour maps, settling over time and patient talk.

Dermal fillers are measured in millilitres, but their results have traditionally been judged by eye. That gap causes real problems: patients who feel "nothing happened", doubt over whether a top-up is needed, and uncertainty about how long a product lasts in a particular patient. 3D imaging gives fillers a measurement to match the syringe.

Why fillers are hard to document in 2D

Filler changes are often a few millimetres of projection in areas with soft curves: the midface, temples, tear trough, lips and chin. From a front photo, that change is nearly invisible. From an oblique or profile view, it depends on exactly where the camera was and how the light fell. We show these effects in why 2D before-and-after photos mislead.

The 3D workflow for fillers

1. Baseline scan at consultation

Scan before any marking or numbing cream. Use the 3D model to discuss the plan, rotating to show the area of volume loss. Patients understand "this is where your cheek has flattened" much faster on their own face than on a diagram. See increasing consultation conversion.

2. Annotate the plan

Mark injection zones, planned depth notes or reference points directly on the model. On Edebo, annotation is available on Pro and Premium plans, and markings stay with the patient's 3D record.

3. Optional immediate post-treatment scan

Useful as a reference, but remember it includes swelling and the effect of any local anaesthetic. Do not use it to judge the final result.

4. Review scan at about two weeks

Align the review scan with baseline. The volume colour map shows where the surface moved outward and by how much. Use the measured change, not the immediate post-injection look, as the result.

5. Longevity scans

Scans at 3, 6 and 12 months show how the correction holds in this patient. That turns "when should I come back?" into a data-informed conversation.

Reading volume measurements carefully

  • Surface volume change is not syringe volume. Tissue response, swelling, product hydration and placement depth mean the visible change can differ from the amount injected.
  • Small changes need caution. Published research on phone-based capture shows volume agreement is looser than landmark agreement; treat very small differences as indicative. See how accurate is smartphone 3D face scanning.
  • Consistency is everything. The same expression, head position and lighting at every scan. A slight smile changes midface volume.

Talking numbers with patients

Keep it simple: show the colour map, give one key number, and connect it to the plan. "We planned to restore volume on your left side to match your right. The scan shows the left midface now sits much closer to the right." Then discuss maintenance.

Avoid presenting measurements as a guarantee of longevity, and remember that 3D imaging is a measurement and visualisation tool, not a diagnostic test.

For filler manufacturers

Measured volume change across many patients is also valuable evidence for product teams and medical affairs. We cover that in 3D imaging for aesthetic pharma companies.

Frequently asked questions

Can 3D imaging measure how much filler was injected?

3D imaging measures the change in surface volume between scans, not the product in the syringe. The visible volume change can differ from the injected volume because of tissue response, swelling and product integration.

When should the after scan be taken for fillers?

Many clinicians take an immediate post-treatment scan for reference and a review scan at about two weeks once swelling has settled, with later scans to track longevity.

Why does my filler result look smaller at follow-up?

Early swelling inflates the immediate result. As it settles, the true correction remains. A measured comparison between the settled scan and baseline shows what was actually achieved.
