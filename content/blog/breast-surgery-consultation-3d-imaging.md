Breast surgery consultations involve decisions about volume, shape and symmetry that are inherently three-dimensional. They also involve some of the most sensitive images a clinic will ever hold. 3D imaging can help with the first, as long as the clinic takes the second seriously.

## Why 3D suits breast consultations

- **Asymmetry is the norm.** Most patients have some difference in volume, position or chest wall shape between sides. Documenting it before surgery is essential. See [facial symmetry analysis](/blog/facial-symmetry-analysis-3d/) for how mirroring works; the same principle applies to the torso.
- **Volume is central to the decision**, whether for augmentation, reduction or reconstruction planning.
- **Photos vary with posture and arm position**, making before-and-after comparison unreliable.

## What 3D imaging can show

On a 3D torso scan, the surgeon can review:

- overall chest shape and projection from any angle,
- **side-to-side volume difference**, as a surface-based estimate,
- **position** of key landmarks relative to each other and the chest wall,
- **contour change** between pre-operative and post-operative scans.

These are surface measurements. They support, but do not replace, clinical examination and the surgeon's judgement. 3D imaging is a measurement and visualisation tool, not a diagnostic device.

## A consultation workflow

1. **Consent first**: explain why the scan is taken, who will see it and how it will be stored. Record specific consent before capture.
2. **Scan** with the patient standing, feet on floor marks, arms in a consistent position, in private.
3. **Review together**, at a pace the patient is comfortable with. Many patients find seeing their shape objectively helpful; some do not want to look closely, and that should be respected.
4. **Document asymmetry** and discuss what surgery can and cannot change.
5. **Record agreed goals** in the notes.

## Post-operative documentation

Swelling and implant settling change shape over months. Scheduled scans let the surgeon track the result as it matures and show the patient measured progress against baseline. Agree the schedule at the time of surgery.

## Privacy is not optional

Breast images are among the most sensitive data a clinic holds. Under India's DPDP Act, clinics must protect personal data with reasonable security safeguards and use it only for the purpose consented to. Practical rules:

- **Never** store these images on personal phones or messaging apps. See [stop storing patient photos on staff phones](/blog/stop-storing-patient-photos-on-staff-phones/).
- **Separate consent** for clinical documentation and for any other use; marketing use of breast images should be exceptional and explicitly consented.
- **Role-based access**, so only the treating team can view.
- **Access logs**, so every view is recorded.
- **Clear deletion** on request, subject to medical record retention requirements.

## Capture tips for torso scans

Torso scans need consistent conditions to be comparable:

- Patient standing, weight even on both feet, on floor marks.
- Arms in the same position each time, for example relaxed at the sides or hands on hips.
- Normal breathing, captured at the end of a relaxed breath out.
- Even lighting around the patient, without strong side light.
- A private space, with only the necessary staff present, and the patient told exactly what the capture involves before it starts.

Recording the patient's position and any garments in the notes makes later comparisons straightforward. See our general [capture tips](/blog/3d-scan-lighting-and-capture-tips/).

As with any aesthetic surgery, imaging helps patients understand their starting point and the realistic range of outcomes. Avoid presenting simulations as promises.
