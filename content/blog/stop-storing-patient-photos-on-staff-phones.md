Walk into many Indian clinics and you will find patient before-and-after photos in the camera roll of a doctor's or assistant's personal phone, shared over WhatsApp groups, and backed up to a personal cloud account. It is convenient. It is also one of the biggest privacy risks a clinic carries.

## How images end up on personal phones

- The clinic never provided a dedicated device or system.
- It is quick: snap, send to the doctor, done.
- Staff share images in WhatsApp groups to discuss cases.
- Nobody told them not to.

None of this is malicious. But it puts highly sensitive images outside the clinic's control.

## What can go wrong

### Loss or theft

A lost phone with hundreds of patient faces and bodies on it is a breach. Many personal phones have weak lock screens.

### Staff leaving

When an employee leaves, the clinic's patient images often leave with them, on their phone and in their cloud backup.

### Automatic backups

Phone galleries sync to personal cloud accounts, often stored abroad, under the employee's control, not the clinic's.

### Forwarding

A single forward in a group chat can spread an image beyond any possibility of recall.

### Mixing with personal content

Patient images sit next to family photos, appear in "memories" features and can be shown accidentally.

### No way to honour patient rights

If a patient asks for deletion, how does the clinic find and delete every copy across staff phones and chats? It usually cannot.

## Why this matters under the DPDP Act

Under the DPDP Act, 2023, the clinic is the data fiduciary for patient images. It must take reasonable security safeguards, use data only for the consented purpose, report breaches and honour requests for erasure. Failure to take reasonable security safeguards falls in the Act's highest penalty band. Most of these obligations apply from May 2027 under the DPDP Rules, 2025. Read [the DPDP Act and patient photos](/blog/dpdp-act-patient-photos-aesthetic-clinics/).

Images scattered across personal phones and chats make every one of those duties hard to meet.

## A practical plan to fix it

### Week 1: Find the images

Ask staff, without blame, where patient images currently live: personal phones, WhatsApp groups, laptops, drives, cloud folders.

### Week 2: Provide the right tool

Give each consult room a clinic-owned device and a system that captures images straight into the patient record, without leaving copies in the phone gallery.

### Week 3: Migrate and delete

Move existing images into the secure system, linked to patient records, then delete copies from personal devices and chats. Record that this was done.

### Week 4: Set the rule and train

Write a one-page policy: no patient images on personal devices, no sharing in messaging apps, discussions about cases happen inside the clinic system. Train every staff member and get written acknowledgement.

### Ongoing

- Check new joiners are trained.
- Remove access and confirm deletion when staff leave.
- Audit a sample of devices periodically.

## What a good system looks like

| Need | What to look for |
|---|---|
| Capture | Images go straight to the patient record, not the gallery |
| Security | Encryption in transit and at rest |
| Access | Role-based, with every view logged |
| Separation | Images stored apart from names and contact details |
| Location | Stored and processed in India |
| Rights | Easy to find and delete all images of a patient |
| Consent | Recorded with each capture |
