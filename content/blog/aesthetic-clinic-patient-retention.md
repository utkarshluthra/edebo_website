In aesthetic medicine, the first treatment is rarely where a clinic makes its money. Programmes, maintenance and complementary treatments over years are. A clinic that wins a patient once and loses them after one session is effectively paying acquisition cost again and again. Retention is the quiet engine of a profitable practice.

## Why patients leave

When clinics ask lapsed patients why they stopped, a few reasons dominate:

1. **"I didn't see much difference."** Often the result was real but undocumented or poorly explained.
2. **"I wasn't sure it was worth it."** The patient could not connect what they paid to what they got.
3. **"Nobody followed up."** No booked review, no reminder, no reason to return.
4. **"Someone else was cheaper."** Price wins when nothing else differentiates the clinic.
5. **A single poor experience**: waiting time, a rushed consult, a billing surprise.

The first two are both about **evidence of progress**. That is where most clinics have the biggest gap.

## The retention system

### 1. Baseline every patient

No treatment that changes shape, volume or skin starts without a Day 0 record. For shape and volume, that means a 3D scan; for skin, a scan with consistent skin analysis. See [standardised before-and-after photos](/blog/standardised-before-after-photos-clinic/).

### 2. Book the review before they leave

A booked follow-up is far more likely to happen than a promised one. See [tracking patient progress across follow-up visits](/blog/tracking-patient-progress-follow-up-visits/).

### 3. Show measured progress at every review

Show their before and after side by side in 3D, with a colour map and one key number. Patients who see their own measured change rarely say "nothing happened". See [measuring aesthetic results in millimetres](/blog/measuring-aesthetic-results-in-millimetres/).

### 4. Agree the next step in the room

The review is where the next session or maintenance plan should be agreed, while the patient is looking at their progress.

### 5. Give them their record

With consent, share a report link the patient can revisit at home. On Edebo, patient report share links do not expire, so the record stays available.

### 6. Remind with relevance

"Your three-month review is due; let's see how your result is holding" works better than a generic reminder.

### 7. Recover lapsed patients

Every quarter, contact patients who have not returned in their expected window, with a relevant reason to come back, not just a discount.

## Packages that support retention

Packages work when the value is visible at each step:

- Break programmes into milestones with a scan at each.
- Price maintenance plans clearly, linked to how long results typically hold for that patient.
- Avoid packages so large that patients feel trapped; they damage trust.

## KPIs to watch monthly

| KPI | What it tells you |
|---|---|
| Repeat visit rate | Share of patients who return within the expected window |
| Programme completion rate | Courses completed ÷ courses started |
| Maintenance booking rate | Patients on a maintenance plan after their initial result |
| Review attendance rate | Booked reviews attended |
| Revenue from returning patients | Share of monthly revenue |
| Lapsed recovery rate | Lapsed patients who return after outreach |
