Every investment in a clinic should earn its keep. 3D imaging is no different. The good news is that its return is easy to model, because it touches a few well-understood numbers: how many consultations convert, how many patients renew, and what a package is worth.

## The model

Monthly return from 3D imaging comes from three sources:

1. **Extra conversions**: more consultations becoming treatment plans because patients understand their concern and the plan. See [increasing consultation conversion](/blog/increase-aesthetic-consultation-conversion/).
2. **Extra renewals**: more patients continuing programmes or booking maintenance because they can see measured progress. See [patient retention](/blog/aesthetic-clinic-patient-retention/).
3. **Fewer disputes and redos**: less time and goodwill lost to "nothing changed" conversations.

The formula:

> **Monthly return** = (extra conversions + extra renewals) × average package margin
>
> **Monthly ROI** = (monthly return − monthly cost) ÷ monthly cost

## Step 1: Your inputs

| Input | Where to find it |
|---|---|
| Consultations per month | Appointment system |
| Current conversion rate | Booked within 30 days ÷ consultations |
| Review visits per month | Appointment system |
| Current renewal rate | Renewals after review ÷ reviews |
| Average package value | Billing |
| Margin on that package | Billing minus product and consumable cost |
| Monthly cost of the system | Vendor pricing |

## Step 2: A conservative uplift

Use a small, conservative uplift for planning, then measure the real number. For example, assume a few percentage points of improvement in conversion and renewal, not a doubling.

## Step 3: Worked example

A single-doctor aesthetic clinic:

- 60 consultations a month, converting at 35% → 21 new plans.
- 40 review visits a month, renewing at 40% → 16 renewals.
- Average package ₹18,000, margin about 60% → ₹10,800 margin per package.
- On Edebo Pro at ₹4,999 a month.

Assume a modest 5-point improvement in each rate:

- Conversion 35% → 40% = 3 extra plans.
- Renewal 40% → 45% = 2 extra renewals.
- **5 extra packages × ₹10,800 = ₹54,000 extra monthly margin.**

Against a ₹4,999 subscription, that is a return of roughly ten times the cost. Even if the real uplift is a fraction of this, the break-even point is low: **one ₹18,000 package covers about 3.6 months of the Pro plan.**

Your numbers will differ. The point is that break-even needs very few additional packages.

## Step 4: Measure the real effect

Do not trust the model; test it:

1. **Baseline**: record conversion and renewal by treatment for one or two months.
2. **Introduce** 3D scans at every consultation and review for target treatments, such as fillers and skin programmes.
3. **Compare** the same metrics for the next two to three months.
4. **Control for seasonality** by comparing with the same months last year if you can.
5. **Ask patients** at booking what influenced their decision.

- The subscription.
- Staff time: a few seconds per scan, plus training. See [capture tips](/blog/3d-scan-lighting-and-capture-tips/).

## Returns that do not show up in the formula

- **Stronger records** if a result is ever disputed.
- **Better treatment planning** from knowing how results held in each patient.
- **Clinic positioning**: patients notice a clinic that measures its work.
- **Staff confidence** in showing results.

These are harder to put a number on, but most clinic owners would value them as much as the direct revenue.
