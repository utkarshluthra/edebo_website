Cost is the reason most Indian clinics have never used 3D imaging. For years it meant a capital purchase, priced in dollars or euros, plus a dedicated room. This article lays out what each option really costs, so you can decide with numbers rather than a sales pitch.

All Edebo prices below are from edebo.in and exclude taxes. For other systems we describe cost structures rather than quote figures, because rig pricing varies by configuration and is negotiated privately.

## The real cost of a fixed rig

The sticker price is only the start. Over five years a rig typically carries:

- **Capital cost** of the cameras, mounts, lighting and software, often in USD or EUR, so exposed to exchange rates.
- **Import, installation and calibration**.
- **A dedicated room**: in a metro clinic, floor space has a real monthly rent attached.
- **Annual software licences and service contracts**.
- **Financing cost** if bought on credit.
- **Replacement or upgrade** at the end of the hardware's useful life.
- **One location only**: a second branch means a second rig.

For a large surgical centre this can work. For most dermatology and aesthetic clinics, especially outside metros, the payback period runs past the life of the equipment.

## The cost of a phone-based subscription

A smartphone system shifts cost from capital to operating expense. You already own the phone. Edebo's plans:

| Plan | Price per month | Scan allowance | Devices |
|---|---|---|---|
| Starter | ₹1,999 | 1x Starter allowance | 1 at a time |
| Pro | ₹4,999 | 2x Starter | 1 at a time |
| Premium | ₹9,999 | 10x Starter | 2 at a time |
| Clinics and platforms | ₹25,000 | 5,000 scans included | 10 profiles, 4 devices each |

On the clinics and platforms plan, scans beyond 5,000 in a month are billed in slabs: ₹10 each up to 25,000, ₹8 up to 1,00,000, and ₹6 above that. Annual billing gives two months free on practitioner plans. Every scan includes the skin analysis report at no extra charge.

There is no room to build, no import, and adding a branch means adding a login or device rather than buying hardware. We discuss the branch question in [standardising imaging across clinic branches](/blog/standardising-imaging-across-clinic-branches/).

## How to model payback

The useful question is not "what does it cost?" but "what does it pay for?" Use four inputs:

1. **Monthly cost** of the system.
2. **Average package value** for the treatments where 3D will be used. Many clinics price filler or skin programmes in the ₹15,000 to ₹25,000 range; use your own number.
3. **Additional conversions per month** you expect from better consultations.
4. **Additional renewals per month** from clearer follow-up results.

Then:

> Monthly return = (extra conversions + extra renewals) × average package value

**Worked example.** A clinic on the Pro plan (₹4,999) with an average package of ₹18,000 needs just one additional package every few months to cover the subscription, because one package equals about 3.6 months of the plan. Anything above that is margin. We go deeper, including how to measure the uplift honestly, in the [ROI of 3D imaging for aesthetic clinics](/blog/roi-of-3d-imaging-aesthetic-clinic/).

## Hidden costs to check with any vendor

- **Per-report or per-export fees**: are reports, skin analysis or PDF exports charged separately?
- **Seat limits**: how many doctors and devices are included?
- **Currency**: is pricing in INR, or will it move with the dollar?
- **Data exit**: can you export your scans if you leave?
- **Compliance work**: if data is stored abroad, your obligations under the DPDP Act may grow. See [the DPDP Act and patient photos](/blog/dpdp-act-patient-photos-aesthetic-clinics/).

## The decision in one line

If your clinic has a dedicated imaging room, a research programme and capital to spare, a rig is a defensible choice. If you want 3D in every consult room and every branch without capital risk, a subscription is the lower-risk path.
