Pigmentation concerns, including melasma, post-inflammatory hyperpigmentation, sun spots and uneven tone, are among the most common reasons Indian patients visit a dermatologist. They are also slow to change, prone to relapse and very sensitive to how they are photographed. That combination makes honest tracking both difficult and essential.

## What makes pigmentation tricky to document

- **Light colour**: warm light hides brown tones; cool light exaggerates them.
- **Camera processing**: phones apply automatic white balance, HDR and beauty smoothing that change apparent pigmentation.
- **Makeup and sunscreen tint**: both mask pigmentation.
- **Season and sun exposure**: summer can worsen pigmentation regardless of treatment.
- **Skin type**: in darker skin types, small changes in contrast are harder to see by eye.

## A capture protocol for pigmentation

1. **Same room, same light source, same colour temperature** every time.
2. **Turn off** beauty modes, filters and portrait effects on the device.
3. **Bare, cleansed skin**. Ask patients to arrive without makeup or tinted sunscreen, or cleanse in clinic.
4. **Standard views**: frontal and both obliques, head level.
5. **Same time relative to treatment**: capture before procedures, not after.
6. **Record context**: season, recent sun exposure, current topical regimen.

## Talking about progress and relapse

Pigmentation programmes need honest framing:

- **Set expectations early**: improvement is usually gradual, maintenance matters, and relapse with sun exposure or hormonal change is common for melasma.
- **Show the trend**, not just the latest image. A slow downward trend in pigmentation score is encouraging even when the mirror does not show much.
- **Explain setbacks** with context: season, sun exposure, a missed regimen.
- **Agree a maintenance plan** before stopping active treatment.

## Building a review rhythm

Pigmentation changes slowly, so the review rhythm matters as much as the capture. A practical pattern many dermatologists use:

1. **Baseline** before starting any regimen, with skin analysis scores recorded.
2. **Early review** after the first few weeks, mainly to check tolerance and adherence, not to judge results.
3. **Progress reviews** at regular intervals, each with the same capture setup and a score comparison.
4. **Transition review** when moving from active treatment to maintenance.
5. **Seasonal check** before summer, when relapse risk often rises.

At each review, note adherence to sunscreen and topicals. When scores stall, the cause is often adherence or sun exposure rather than the treatment itself, and the record makes that conversation factual rather than accusatory.

## Indian skin considerations

Indian patients span a wide range of skin types, and post-inflammatory hyperpigmentation after acne, procedures or irritation is common. That has two implications for tracking:

- **Track procedure-related pigmentation separately**, so that a temporary darkening after a peel or laser is not confused with relapse of the original concern.
- **Compare each patient with themselves**, never with a population average. Scores are most meaningful as a personal trend under identical capture conditions.

Clinical judgement decides what is happening and what to do next; consistent records make sure that judgement is based on more than memory.

## Why consistent tracking improves retention

Pigmentation patients are among the most likely to drop out early, because change is slow. A visible, consistent trend at each visit gives them a reason to continue, and gives the clinic a fair way to show the value of a maintenance plan.
