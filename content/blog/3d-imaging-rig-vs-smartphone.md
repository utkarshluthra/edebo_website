Once a clinic decides it wants 3D imaging, the next decision is what kind. The market splits into three families: fixed multi-camera rigs, handheld scanners, and smartphone-based systems. Each makes sense for a different clinic. This guide lays out the trade-offs honestly.

## The three families

### Fixed stereophotogrammetry rigs

Several calibrated cameras mounted on arms, with controlled flash lighting, capture the face in one instant. Well-known examples include Canfield's Vectra range and 3dMD systems.

**Strengths**: very well validated, highly standardised, instant capture (no motion), strong research track record.

**Trade-offs**: high upfront cost, usually priced in USD or EUR; needs a dedicated room; tied to one location; annual service and software licences.

### Handheld structured-light scanners

A handheld device projects a light pattern and measures the distortion to build a surface.

**Strengths**: portable, good detail, useful for body and limb scanning.

**Trade-offs**: specialist hardware to buy and maintain; capture takes practice; often designed for engineering use rather than clinic workflows.

### Smartphone-based systems

Software uses the phone's camera, sometimes with its depth sensor, to build a 3D model. We explain the method in [how smartphone 3D scanning works](/blog/how-smartphone-3d-scanning-works/).

**Strengths**: no hardware purchase; works in any room with adequate light; easy to add a second device or branch; subscription pricing.

**Trade-offs**: capture takes a few seconds of movement, so the patient must stay still; quality depends on lighting and technique; app quality varies widely across the market.

## Which clinic fits which system

**A rig makes sense if** you run a large surgical centre with a dedicated imaging room, you publish research, and capital budget is not the constraint.

**A handheld scanner makes sense if** you do a lot of body or limb work and have staff who will become skilled operators.

**A smartphone system makes sense if** you are a dermatology or aesthetic clinic, a single surgeon practice, a chain that wants the same standard in every branch, or a clinic in a Tier 2 or Tier 3 city where a rig's cost will never be recovered. ## A buyer's checklist

Whatever you choose, ask every vendor these questions:

1. **Accuracy**: what reference system was it compared with, what metric, what sample size, and is it published?
2. **Repeatability**: how close are two scans of the same face taken minutes apart?
3. **Coverage**: face only, or hairline and body too?
4. **Measurements**: volume, symmetry, distances, contour? Are they automatic or manual?
5. **Follow-up comparison**: can it align Day 0 and Day 30 scans and show change?
6. **Consent and privacy**: is consent recorded with each scan? Where is data stored? Is it used to train models? How is deletion handled? Read [the DPDP Act and patient photos](/blog/dpdp-act-patient-photos-aesthetic-clinics/) before you sign.
7. **Integration**: does it connect to your EMR or CRM? See [integrating 3D imaging with clinic CRM and EMR](/blog/integrating-3d-imaging-clinic-crm-emr/).
8. **Total cost over five years**, including service, licences and replacement.
9. **Support**: local support hours and language.
