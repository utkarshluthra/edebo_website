Most aesthetic clinics spend heavily to bring patients through the door, through social media, aggregators, referrals and events, and then lose a large share of them in the consultation room. Improving conversion is usually cheaper than buying more leads. Here is a structured way to do it.

## Start by measuring

You cannot improve what you do not track. Record for every consultation:

- lead source,
- treatment discussed,
- doctor,
- outcome: booked, considering, declined,
- reason if declined,
- follow-up attempts and result.

Calculate **conversion rate = treatments booked within 30 days ÷ consultations**, by treatment and by doctor. This is your baseline. Every change below should be tested against it.

## Why consultations fail

When clinics review declined consultations honestly, the same reasons recur:

1. **The patient did not understand the problem.** They came in for "tired eyes" and left without seeing why.
2. **They could not picture the plan.** A verbal description of filler in the midface means little.
3. **Pricing was confusing** or appeared only at the end.
4. **Trust was low**: generic before-and-after images that might be anyone.
5. **No follow-up** after the patient said "I'll think about it".

## A consultation structure that converts

### 1. Listen first

Ask what bothers them, in their words, and when they notice it. Write down the exact phrase.

### 2. Show them their own face

This is where visual tools matter. Scan the patient and rotate their own 3D face to show the concern from the angle they described. A patient who sees their own midface volume loss in 3D grasps it in seconds. Read [what is 3D facial imaging](/blog/what-is-3d-facial-imaging/) for the basics.

### 3. Explain the cause simply

One or two sentences on why it happens, using the model.

### 4. Present one clear plan

Lead with the recommended plan, not a menu of ten options. Mark the treatment areas on the model. Explain what change to expect and when, without promising outcomes. See [managing patient expectations](/blog/managing-patient-expectations-aesthetics/).

### 5. Show how progress will be measured

"We'll scan you again in two weeks and show you exactly what changed." This reduces the perceived risk of saying yes. See [measuring aesthetic results in millimetres](/blog/measuring-aesthetic-results-in-millimetres/).

### 6. Make pricing clear

Total price, what is included, number of sessions, and payment options. Surprises at the end kill conversion.

### 7. Give them something to take home

A summary of the plan, and with consent, a link to their own scan or report. Patients often decide at home with family; give them something concrete to show.

## Follow up properly

Many "I'll think about it" patients convert with a timely, helpful follow-up:

- A message within 48 hours with the plan summary.
- An answer to the specific concern they raised.
- One more reminder a week later. Then stop.

## Train and review

- Review conversion by doctor monthly, and share what the best performers do differently.
- Role-play the consultation structure with new staff.
- Review a sample of declined consultations each month.

## KPIs to track

| KPI | Definition |
|---|---|
| Consultation conversion | Booked within 30 days ÷ consultations |
| Conversion by lead source | Shows which channels bring serious patients |
| Average plan value | Revenue per converted consultation |
| Follow-up conversion | Converted after follow-up ÷ "considering" |
| Time to decision | Days from consultation to booking |

Better consultations feed directly into retention and lifetime value; see [aesthetic clinic patient retention](/blog/aesthetic-clinic-patient-retention/) and model the return in [the ROI of 3D imaging](/blog/roi-of-3d-imaging-aesthetic-clinic/).
